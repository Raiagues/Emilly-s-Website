Fundação Estudar appears in so many of my stories that it deserves one of its own. I have fewer photos than I would like, but I remember the conversations very well. It was there that I started looking beyond my space engineering bubble and meeting people whose paths made me reconsider what might be possible for me too.

Scholarships and recognitions along the way

01

The first time, I didn’t even get an interview

I first applied in 2022 and was rejected before being invited to an interview. But the application had already made me think. Trying to understand what the foundation was looking for made me look more closely at the impact I wanted my work to have.

I wasn’t trying to arrange my life around being accepted one day. I genuinely identified with those questions. What did I want to build? Who would it help? I kept living my life, but I was starting to understand my own reasons more clearly, even before becoming part of the community.

02

I needed the scholarship

When I applied again in 2025, I was in France and preparing to return to Brazil. I needed financial support to continue my studies. That was a very real part of my decision. At that point, I still couldn’t imagine how much the community would come to mean to me.

My selection process lasted roughly from February to August. There were behavioural interviews, technical interviews, individual conversations and group stages, all alongside university. It was demanding because I had to do much more than fill in forms: I had to think about my choices, understand my results and find a way to explain my story.

In the individual interviews, I also tried to ask questions. One interviewer, who was studying at Harvard at the time, told me he couldn’t see a downside to being part of the foundation. That stayed with me. As I learned more from the people interviewing me, I found more reasons of my own for wanting to join.

03

The call after midnight

The final panel would normally have been in person, but they let me take part online because I was still in France. Several evaluators and other people were watching, and I was the first to answer. Somehow, I felt confident. After months of taking the process seriously, I knew why I wanted to be there and could explain the numbers behind my work.

It was already around midnight in France when it ended. I tried to sleep and couldn’t. Then I got hungry, got up and noticed all the missed calls on my phone. I called back. It was someone from the foundation: the evaluation was over, and I had been accepted.

I didn’t know what to say. My face actually hurt from smiling so much. I still find it hard to describe that moment, but I remember the feeling very clearly.

The 2025 fellows

04

Learning to be in a different room

I had spent so much time around the space sector that meeting people from completely different fields was an education in itself. I was learning to take part in conversations where I wasn’t surrounded by people with the same technical background as me.

The support became very concrete: being able to message someone, ask for help, hear about an opportunity or talk through something I was trying to do. And being around ambitious people changed my own sense of possibility. I knew I had a good technical foundation. Seeing people with backgrounds I could relate to doing remarkable things made me think I could try more too.

05

Conversations I couldn’t have imagined

I couldn’t attend the in-person onboarding because I was in France, but I did go to the annual event in São Paulo. We had the chance to speak directly with the foundation’s founders: Beto Sicupira, Marcel Telles and Jorge Paulo Lemann.

I spent a long time talking with Beto and Marcel. Later, I had lunch with Marcel and other fellows, and the conversation continued. He even tried to connect me with a founder he thought I should meet. I hadn’t imagined being able to have those conversations, sitting around the same table.

Meeting the other fellows mattered just as much. It was a chance to put faces, voices and stories to that community I had joined from a distance. Little by little, I was learning how to be part of it.

About Fundação Estudar

06

Lyfe Academy and mentorship

Soon after joining the foundation in 2025, I started receiving the community emails. One of the first opportunities I saw was the selection process for Lyfe Academy, a programme focused on personal and professional development, with mentorship. I applied and joined.

We spent about three days at a countryside hotel in São Paulo state. I met people from different fields, made friends and kept in touch with the group throughout the year. There were conversations about our futures, but also karaoke and time in the pool. Those moments are part of how I remember the programme.

Lyfe matched me with Verônica Allende Serra as my mentor. She helped me in many ways, and one of our conversations later led to the space panel at South Summit. That story has its own chapter in this diary; this is where it began.

07

The part I couldn’t finish

I didn’t complete Lyfe Academy. The later part overlapped with CERN and the end of my degree, and I had a very heavy workload. I want to be clear about that when I talk about the programme: I took part, learned a lot and made meaningful connections, but I didn’t finish it.

That is part of the story too. Being excited about opportunities doesn’t make time unlimited. I can value what I experienced there and be honest about what I was able to take on during that period.

I applied because I needed a scholarship. Today, when I think about Fundação Estudar, I also think about the people I can reach out to and the things I now allow myself to try. I don’t think I needed to arrive with my whole future worked out. Taking the application seriously helped me understand myself better; being part of the community keeps expanding the questions I ask about what comes next.
