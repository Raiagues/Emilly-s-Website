My experience with Fundação Estudar

The scholarship selection, meeting the community and the opportunities that followed.

Fundação Estudar appears in so many of my stories that it deserves one of its own. I have fewer photos than I would like, but I remember the conversations very well. It was there that I started looking beyond my space engineering bubble and meeting people whose paths made me reconsider what might be possible for me too.

Scholarships and recognitions along the way

01

The first application

I first applied in 2022 and was rejected before being invited to an interview. But the application had already made me think. Trying to understand what the foundation was looking for made me look more closely at the impact I wanted my work to have.

I wasn’t trying to arrange my life around being accepted one day. I genuinely identified with those questions. What did I want to build? Who would it help? I kept living my life, but I was starting to understand my own reasons more clearly, even before becoming part of the community.

02

The 2025 selection process

I applied again in 2025, while preparing to return from France to Brazil. I was seeking support to continue my studies and wanted to join the Foundation’s community.

The process lasted roughly from February to August, with technical and behavioural interviews, individual conversations and group stages. Alongside university, I prepared to explain my choices, responsibilities and project results clearly.

03

The final interview and acceptance

I took part in the final interview online because I was still in France. Preparing for the earlier stages helped me discuss my technical work and the results behind it with the panel.

My acceptance was confirmed after the final assessment. Receiving the scholarship was an important recognition of the work I had developed during university and supported the next stage of my studies.

The 2025 fellows

04

Exchanging ideas across fields

I had spent so much time around the space sector that meeting people from completely different fields was an education in itself. I was learning to take part in conversations where I wasn’t surrounded by people with the same technical background as me.

The support became very concrete. Being able to message someone, ask for help, hear about an opportunity or talk through something I was trying to do. And being around ambitious people changed my own sense of possibility. I knew I had a good technical foundation. Seeing people with backgrounds I could relate to doing remarkable things made me think I could try more too.

05

The annual gathering in São Paulo

At the annual gathering in São Paulo, I met other scholars and spoke with the Foundation’s founders, Beto Sicupira, Marcel Telles and Jorge Paulo Lemann. The conversations covered our projects and different professional experiences.

I also had lunch with Marcel and other scholars. I appreciated the chance to present what I was working on and hear how people from other fields approached their projects.

About Fundação Estudar

06

Lyfe Academy and mentorship

Soon after joining the foundation in 2025, I started receiving the community emails. One of the first opportunities I saw was the selection process for Lyfe Academy, a programme focused on personal and professional development, with mentorship. I applied and joined.

We spent about three days at a countryside hotel in São Paulo state. I met people from different fields, made friends and kept in touch with the group throughout the year. There were conversations about our futures, but also karaoke and time in the pool. Those moments are part of how I remember the programme.

Lyfe matched me with Verônica Allende Serra as my mentor. She helped me in many ways, and one of our conversations later led to the space panel at South Summit. That story has its own chapter in this diary. This is where it began.

07

My participation in the programme

I took part in Lyfe Academy but did not complete the programme. Its final stage overlapped with CERN and the end of my degree. I retain the learning and mentoring from the period I attended.

That is part of the story too. Being excited about opportunities doesn’t make time unlimited. I can value what I experienced there and be honest about what I was able to take on during that period.

Par avion · What I learned

Taking the application seriously helped me understand what I wanted to build and why. Being part of the community keeps changing the questions I ask about what comes next. I do not have that future completely worked out, but I now have people to exchange ideas with, ask for advice and celebrate each other’s projects. That is a part of the scholarship I could not yet picture when I applied.
