Visiona · back in Brazil · October 2025 to June 2026

Flight software and systems engineering at Visiona

The return to Brazil, work with the team and a closer look at satellite integration and testing.

StoryBrazilOctober 2025 to June 2026
In this story
Returning to BrazilWorking with the teamBrazil’s space industryAn interest in systems engineeringA visit to INPE’s integration and testing laboratoryTime with the team

I joined Visiona just after returning to Brazil from France. I arrived with everything I had learned abroad, but there was still so much I didn’t know about the space industry in my own country. That internship changed the way I saw it, and helped me understand what I enjoyed doing as an engineer.

My technical work at Visiona

01

Returning to Brazil

The interview was in Portuguese, of course. But I had just come back from spending my days speaking French or English. My head hadn’t quite switched languages yet.

Much of what I knew about embedded systems and working with satellites, I had learned in France. Sometimes I knew exactly what I wanted to explain, but the technical term would only come to me in French. It was strange to be back in my own country, trying to find words in my own language.

02

Working with the team

I worked remotely and spent some time with the team in person. Early conversations with colleagues and supervisors helped me understand the company’s projects and how my work fitted into them.

03

Brazil’s space industry

I had already worked on a Brazilian satellite project, but I hadn’t seen this side of the industry from within. At Visiona, I met people with extraordinary technical knowledge. Seeing what that team could build made me reconsider assumptions I hadn’t even realised I carried about technology in Brazil.

One milestone in that history was VCUB1, launched in 2023. A pioneering Earth-observation and data-collection nanosatellite designed by Brazilian industry.

In 2023, Finep announced approximately R$220 million for a high-resolution Earth-observation satellite project led by Visiona, its largest economic subsidy award at that time.

Working with the team helped me understand how flight software fits into the other parts of a satellite project.

Visiona · the company’s history

Finep · funding announcement, 2023

04

An interest in systems engineering

I worked on flight software, but I also learned a lot about systems engineering, integration and satellite operations. I started becoming interested in how everything connected.

Because of my background, I had thought embedded software was the path I needed to stay on. Then I realised how much I enjoyed testing, reviewing cases, thinking through what could go wrong and finding errors. I liked having to question things and look carefully at the less obvious situations.

That interest in systems engineering grew almost naturally. Visiona helped me see it in the work I enjoyed doing every day.

05

A visit to INPE’s integration and testing laboratory

During my time on site, I visited INPE’s Integration and Testing Laboratory, LIT. I saw thermal vacuum chambers, the vibration test bench and the anechoic chamber, and followed part of the satellite integration work.

Seeing the facilities helped connect the work on software and systems with the physical verification of a satellite. The photos here show the laboratory facilities.

INPE · Integration and Testing Laboratory

06

Time with the team

Being on site also gave me more time with colleagues outside meetings and technical tasks. I kept some photos of those moments and of outings during that period in Brazil.

Par avion · What I learned

I had arrived thinking that my background pointed mainly towards embedded software. The everyday work helped me notice something else. How much I enjoyed asking what could go wrong, testing and understanding how the parts fitted together. That interest in systems engineering grew through doing the work. I also left with a much closer view of Brazil’s space industry and more questions I wanted to explore.
