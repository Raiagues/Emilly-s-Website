Visiona · back in Brazil · October 2025–June 2026

Visiona: seeing Brazil differently

An interview with French words in my head, a team that welcomed me and a growing interest in systems engineering.

I joined Visiona just after returning to Brazil from France. I arrived with everything I had learned abroad, but there was still so much I didn’t know about the space industry in my own country. That internship changed the way I saw it, and helped me understand what I enjoyed doing as an engineer.

My technical work at Visiona →

01

An interview in Portuguese, a head full of French

The interview was in Portuguese, of course. But I had just come back from spending my days speaking French or English. My head hadn’t quite switched languages yet.

Much of what I knew about embedded systems and working with satellites, I had learned in France. Sometimes I knew exactly what I wanted to explain, but the technical term would only come to me in French. It was strange to be back in my own country, trying to find words in my own language.

02

A team that welcomed me

The team was welcoming from the very beginning. The internship would normally have been on site, but they made an exception so I could work remotely. I learned so much there. During my holidays, I went to spend some time with them in person.

A big part of those first weeks was talking to my supervisors and listening to how the company’s projects had come together. I wanted to understand the path they had taken to get there.

03

The technology we have here

I had already worked on a Brazilian satellite project, but I hadn’t seen this side of the industry from within. At Visiona, I met people with extraordinary technical knowledge. Seeing what that team could build made me reconsider assumptions I hadn’t even realised I carried about technology in Brazil.

One milestone in that history was VCUB1, launched in 2023: a pioneering Earth-observation and data-collection nanosatellite designed by Brazilian industry.

In 2023, Finep announced approximately R$220 million for a high-resolution Earth-observation satellite project led by Visiona, its largest economic subsidy award at that time.

And I was there, contributing to a project on that scale. Little by little, I began to understand how my work fitted into something much bigger. That gave what I was doing a different meaning.

04

Finding my way towards systems engineering

I worked on flight software, but I also learned a lot about systems engineering, integration and satellite operations. I started becoming interested in how everything connected.

Because of my background, I had thought embedded software was the path I needed to stay on. Then I realised how much I enjoyed testing, reviewing cases, thinking through what could go wrong and finding errors. I liked having to question things and look carefully at the less obvious situations.

That interest in systems engineering grew almost naturally. Visiona helped me see it in the work I enjoyed doing every day.

05

Seeing the LIT in person

During my time on site, I visited INPE’s Integration and Testing Laboratory, the LIT. It provides facilities for assembling, integrating and qualifying space systems, including tests that reproduce conditions experienced in space.

Seeing the thermal-vacuum chambers, the vibration test equipment and the anechoic chamber up close was incredible. The size of the large thermal-vacuum chamber really struck me. I also got to see satellite integration work happening in person.

I already worked in the field, and there was still so much technical capability in Brazil that I hadn’t known about. I left wanting to understand it better. I can’t share the satellite images or internal details, but these photos of the facilities bring back that feeling of discovery.

06

And the time together outside work

There was also time together outside work, like this games night. For someone who worked remotely, being there in person meant a lot. I came back with new things I had learned, memories with the team and a much closer view of the industry I wanted to be part of.

Outside Visiona, I also made friends in the house where I was staying. We were different ages and came from very different fields, and I loved getting to know people whose everyday lives were so different from mine. They became close friends.

We went karting in the rain and spent time in parks. These photos belong here too: staying there gave me the chance to get closer to the work, but also to share a little of my life with people I would probably never have met through engineering alone.

What stays with me

I learned a lot at Visiona about satellites, about Brazil and about the kind of work that makes me curious. I loved getting to see all of that up close.
