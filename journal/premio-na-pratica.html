I applied with about two or three days left before the deadline. I didn’t think I had much chance of winning, and going to China wasn’t even something I imagined for myself. Looking back, I’m so glad I filled in that application.

Awards and press

01

Looking back at my own story

I heard about the Prêmio Na Prática Protagonismo Universitário through Fundação Estudar. They shared the opportunity with our community. At first, I wasn’t going to apply. Then, very close to the deadline, I decided to try.

The process itself was already worth it. Writing about my experiences made me notice things I hadn’t stopped to see while I was living them. It wasn’t that I had forgotten what I’d done; I just hadn’t looked at those moments together, with a little distance.

02

The pink plane and arriving in São Paulo

When I reached the in-person final, Na Prática covered the trip to São Paulo: flights, hotel and the costs of taking part. They met us at the airport, and we all stayed in the same hotel, each with our own room. I felt so well looked after.

And I flew on a pink plane! I kept a photo because it was one of those little details that made the whole experience even more fun.

03

A few days at BTG Pactual

The final took place at BTG Pactual. Alongside the final judging panel, we had training, mentoring and plenty of chances to talk to people. I wanted to make the most of being there.

There were people from very different fields. That was part of what made it so good: hearing about experiences that weren’t like mine and learning from them.

04

When they announced the result

The ceremony was on 3 November 2025, and I won the award for the South region. There are photos of me waiting for the result, speaking on stage and holding the trophy. Seeing them still brings that moment back.

Winning was wonderful, of course. But I would also have been happy to see another finalist from the South receive it. They were incredible people with incredible stories. I never experienced those days as a competition between us. I was already so happy just to be there.

The result published by Na Prática

05

The friendships that stayed

There was a party, there were long conversations, and at the hotel we spent evenings in each other’s rooms, chatting and gossiping. We all stayed in the same place, so there was time to get to know each other beyond the programme.

I met people from the space sector and from Fundação Estudar, including friends like Gabi and Nara. Some of those friendships carried on through the trips to China and Brazil at Silicon Valley. It was lovely to see those paths crossing again.

We’re still close. We have a group where we keep talking, and now we’re following the new finalists in the 2026 edition together. It brings back so much of what we lived through.

That is why I find it strange to remember those days only as a competition. Of course I wanted to win, but I also wanted things to go well for the people I was getting to know. Following the new finalists together now brings back that feeling: how good it is to have people around who are excited about what you are trying to do.

06

And then we were on a magazine cover

Then something happened that I hadn’t imagined either: we appeared on the cover of EXAME. My university, UFSM, also published an article about me and my story.

People started stopping me at the supermarket to say they had seen the news and to congratulate me. It was funny: I felt like a celebrity. Such an unexpected, lovely part of it all.

The EXAME feature

My story on the UFSM website

I almost let the deadline pass because I thought I had no chance. Looking back, writing the application was already valuable: I had to stop and notice things I had done without giving myself much credit for them. The award and China were wonderful, and so were the friendships. If you recognise yourself in that hesitation, my advice is to look at what the application actually asks for before ruling yourself out. You may have more to say about your own story than you think.
