My summer at CERN

Selection for openlab, machine learning research for ATLAS and nine weeks in an international laboratory.

I joined CERN openlab in the summer of 2026, on my third application. My place was confirmed after a change to one of the projects. Here I share a little of the research, visits and life with friends during those nine weeks.

01

Preparing for openlab

I was accepted on my third application. For that selection, I studied the programme’s requirements, spoke with former participants and worked on presenting my technical experience clearly.

The first response, on 2 April 2026, was a rejection. On 30 April, after a change in one of the projects, CERN confirmed my place.

02

Selection and arrival

The acceptance email confirmed that I had been selected from nearly 6,000 valid applications for nine weeks at CERN. The project was “Trigger Level Analysis Data Preparation through Co-processors”.

I organised the trip with support from the programme team. Meeting participants from different countries was part of the experience from the start.

03

Research for ATLAS

I worked under the supervision of Ioannis Xiotidis and Noah Clarke Hall on Next-Generation Triggers activities for ATLAS. The question was how to reduce detector data while preserving the information needed for physics analysis.

I compared compact machine learning models with future FPGA implementation in mind. This meant considering both the quality of the results and hardware constraints, as well as the effects of overlapping collisions and the amount of data to transmit.

Some alternatives improved the results in the study. FPGA implementation and hardware measurements remained future work. The report and technical page present the results and their limits.

04

Visits and lectures

I visited ATLAS, ALICE and the Antimatter Factory. Seeing the experiments in person helped put the research in context. The programme also included lectures, among them a class with the creator of C++.

05

Exploring beyond the laboratory

Outside the programme, I went hiking and visited places with other participants. The album includes Barcelona, Einstein’s house in Bern and mountain landscapes. Those outings were a chance to get to know the group beyond work.

06

Learning with the group

Working and spending time with students from different backgrounds was an important part of the summer. Conversations about our projects exposed me to research questions beyond my own and led to friendships I value.

Par avion · What I learned

Those nine weeks brought a new research topic, visits to experiments and plenty of time with friends. I came back with more experience in machine learning for particle physics and good memories from outside the laboratory too.
