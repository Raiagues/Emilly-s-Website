CERN openlab · July to September 2026

My summer at CERN

Selection for openlab, machine learning research for ATLAS and nine weeks in an international laboratory.

StoryGeneva, SwitzerlandJuly to September 2026
In this story

When the CERN openlab email arrived, I thought it was another automatic message. I had already received a rejection in that selection round. Only when I started reading about the project did I realise I had been accepted. This is the story of the nine weeks that followed, in the lab and beyond it.

01

Preparing for openlab

I was accepted on my third application. For that selection, I studied the programme’s requirements, spoke with former participants and worked on presenting my technical experience clearly.

The first response, on 2 April 2026, was a rejection. On 30 April, after a change in one of the projects, CERN confirmed my place.

02

Selection and arrival

On 30 April 2026, I woke up to an email from CERN openlab. I had just got up and, because I had already been rejected, I thought it was another automatic rejection message. It was only when I started reading about the project that I realised what had happened. I had been accepted.

I organised the trip with support from the programme team. Meeting participants from different countries was part of the experience from the start.

Read the email excerpt

An excerpt from the email · 30 April 2026

Successful application to the CERN openlab Summer Student Programme 2026

While you previously received a rejection from us, we are pleased to inform you that, following recent changes in one of our projects, you have now been accepted to the CERN openlab Summer Student Programme 2026
03

Research for ATLAS

I worked under the supervision of Ioannis Xiotidis and Noah Clarke Hall on Next-Generation Triggers activities for ATLAS. The question was how to reduce detector data while preserving the information needed for physics analysis.

I compared compact machine learning models with future FPGA implementation in mind. This meant considering both the quality of the results and hardware constraints, as well as the effects of overlapping collisions and the amount of data to transmit.

Some alternatives improved the results in the study. FPGA implementation and hardware measurements remained future work. The report and technical page present the results and their limits.

04

Visits and lectures

I visited ATLAS, the experiment my work was connected to. I also visited ALICE and the Antimatter Factory. These photos show very different corners of CERN, each with its own equipment and experiments.

And yes, one of those classroom photos is from a lecture with the creator of C++. That definitely belongs in this diary.

05

Exploring beyond the laboratory

Outside the programme, I went hiking and visited places with other participants. The album includes Barcelona, Einstein’s house in Bern and mountain landscapes. Those outings were a chance to get to know the group beyond work.

06

Learning with the group

I met incredible friends during the programme. There are photos at the table, on campus, out together and up in the mountains. I wanted those moments here too, alongside the report and the work. They are part of how I remember my time at CERN.

When I tell someone about CERN, it is easy to start with the experiments and the project. But I also want to remember who was around me during those weeks. The friends, the shared room and the trips belong in this story just as much as the work I went there to do.

Par avion · What I learned

I still think about that email I almost overlooked. Preparing carefully did not guarantee a place. The acceptance also depended on a project change I could not control. What I could do was understand the work, ask questions and take the application seriously. I came back with research experience, but also with friends and memories that belong in the story just as much as the report.
