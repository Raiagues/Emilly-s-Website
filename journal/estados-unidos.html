Fundação Estudar · travel diary · 6–12 April 2026

My first time in the United States

An emergency visa appointment, Silicon Valley, Boston and the people who made it all worth it.

In April 2026, I went to the United States for the first time. I had grown up hearing about Stanford, MIT and Harvard, and suddenly I was there, walking around those campuses. But before any of that, there was a selection email, very little time and a visa to sort out.

01

First, the visa

On February 27, 2026, I received the email confirming I had been selected to attend Brazil at Silicon Valley with funding from Fundação Estudar. I had a little over a month to get my visa. I requested an emergency appointment and started trying to make everything happen in time. It was chaotic.

In California, Anderson and his wife, Juliana, volunteers with Fundação Estudar, welcomed us into their home. Having people receive us like that made this first trip much more special.

02

So many different conversations

Brazil at Silicon Valley brings people from Brazil closer to the technology and entrepreneurship community in Silicon Valley. The 2026 edition took place at Google MP7, in Sunnyvale, from April 6 to 8. And there I was, getting to be part of it.

I talked to founders, investors, researchers and people working in law and AI. I loved that the conversations went so far beyond my own field. There were so many people to meet and so many things I wanted to ask. Somewhere along the way, I became sure I wanted to come back.

03

Places I had only seen from afar

Alongside the events, I visited companies and universities. I got to see Stanford and Berkeley, and we also visited Meta. Being at Stanford was one of those moments when I kept thinking: I am actually here.

At Berkeley, I even photographed a parking space reserved for a Nobel laureate. I wanted to keep all those little details, too.

04

And then, Boston

Since I was already in the US, I decided to go to Brasil Project as well. This part of the trip was my own expense. Fundação Estudar funded my participation in Brazil at Silicon Valley; going on to Boston was a separate decision.

The first edition of Brasil Project took place from April 10 to 12 at Harvard and MIT, in Cambridge, near Boston, bringing together conversations about Brazil, technology, business and public life. I got to visit both universities. I had spent so long seeing these institutions from a distance, and now I could see them in person. It really was a dream coming true.

05

A conversation at AeroAstro

At MIT, I had the chance to talk to Professor Kerri Cahoy, from AeroAstro, about different research areas, including satellites. She leads STAR Lab, which works on space technologies such as small satellites and laser communications.

Getting to have that conversation in person meant a lot to me. Visiting the campus was already exciting; being able to talk about the areas I care about made it even more memorable.

Kerri Cahoy · MIT AeroAstro ↗

06

There was so much life in between

It was my first time in California. Even the yellow school buses were exciting: I had seen them so many times in films, and now they were right there. We rode bikes by the Golden Gate Bridge and went to a basketball game with the Brazilian player Gui Santos.

There were shared meals, walks, time together at the apartment and a lot of laughter. Some of the people I met on this trip are still close to me. When I look at the photos, I remember how much fun we had just being together.

07

Coming home wanting to try more

It was on this trip that I tried Claude for the first time. It already existed, but I had never used it. I remember being amazed that the technology was already at that point.

I came back to Brazil wanting to move faster on my own projects: try something, see how it works and make another version. That was one of the things I brought home, along with the friendships and the very clear feeling that I wanted to go back.

Meeting people from so many fields mattered too. A conversation didn’t have to be about satellites to make me think about my own work. Hearing how other people built things helped me see my projects from outside the engineering perspective I was used to.

What stays with me

Seeing those campuses in person was a dream. What I could bring home, though, was something I could use without being there: the willingness to try an idea sooner, talk to someone who sees it differently and improve it as I learn. I still want to go back. In the meantime, I can put that part of the trip into practice in the projects I already have in front of me.
